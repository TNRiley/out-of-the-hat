Out of the Hat

For fifty years Eurovision drew its running order by lot. In those finals, a song drawn into the last third of the night finished about one and a half places higher than one drawn into the first.

A lottery is an experiment nobody had to design: no producer chose who went late, so if late songs did better, it was the slot and not the song. Every figure below is recalculated in your browser from 2,148 entries in 107 shows, 1957 to 2025, extracted from each contest's own Wikipedia article. And the famous curse of slot two? That is what chance leaves somewhere, and slot sixteen has never won either.

The control

If the hat really was a hat, a country's slot should know nothing about how good that country was before the draw. So the same test is run on something the running order cannot have caused: each country's average finish over its previous five finals. If that leans the way the result leans, the draw was not clean, and the headline above would not stand.

The hat kept changing

The result depends entirely on knowing which years were a lottery, and the rule was not constant. These are the regimes, as each contest's own article describes its draw. Getting this wrong would quietly mix producer decisions into an experiment.

1956

Seven countries, two songs each. Only the winner was ever announced, so there are no places to measure. Left out.

1957–2006

The running order is drawn by lot, typically months ahead (in 1961 a six-year-old and a four-year-old helped). Countries that withdraw after the draw leave a gap the remaining entries close up around.

1996

An audio-only qualifying round: juries heard 29 songs on tape, in a drawn order, and 22 went through. No stage at all.

2007

Five semi-final countries and three finalists win the right to choose their slot. All eight pick the second half of the show. The people with the most at stake believed the folk wisdom.

2008

Again some countries are drawn to pick their own position: three in each semi-final, and Serbia, the host, in the final.

2009–2012

The articles describe a running-order draw and say nothing about choosers either way. Kept with 2007–08 as "lot with exceptions", out of the headline.

2013–

Countries draw only which half they perform in; the show's producers set the exact order. No longer an experiment.

Methods

Sources

  • English Wikipedia, "Eurovision Song Contest 1957" to "Eurovision Song Contest 2025": 69 articles read through the MediaWiki parse API on 2026-10-07. Every result table (running order, country, points, place) and each article's account of how the running order was set. Text and tables are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0; the exact revision of each article is recorded in the repository, so the numbers here can be checked against what was read. The derived data on this page is shared under the same licence.
  • No other data. The original plan was to use a widely shared Eurovision dataset on GitHub; it carries no licence and was scraped from a fan site, so it was set aside and the tables were rebuilt from the articles instead.

What was measured, and how

  • Slot is the entry's rank in the show's running order, so a country that withdrew after the draw does not leave a hole. The Netherlands in 2024 was disqualified before the final and holds no slot. Position within the night is slot minus one over field size minus one: 0 for the opener, 1 for the closer.
  • Measure A is the finishing place as a share of the way down the scoreboard, (place − 1) / (field − 1), so a field of 16 and a field of 26 sit on the same scale. Ties keep the place the article gives them (1969 had four winners).
  • Measure B is whether a semi-final entry reached that year's final. It is derived from the final's own table, not from row colouring, and every semi-final checks out at exactly ten qualifiers.
  • Significance is a permutation test: slots are shuffled within each show, 2,000 times, and the observed correlation is compared with that spread. Shuffling within a show keeps every year's field size and scoring system intact.
  • The control is each country's mean Measure A over its previous five finals (at least two needed), correlated with its slot in the same way. Its verdict is computed live: if it ever falls outside its own chance band, the headline figure is replaced by a warning.
  • The places figure in the headline is the gap in mean (place − 1) / (field − 1) between the first and last thirds, multiplied by the median field size minus one.
  • Parse checks: in every one of the 107 tables, more points never comes with a worse place, and every semi-final yields ten qualifiers. Both checks are in src/shape.py and stop the build if they fail.

What this does not say

  • Not why. Juries tiring, televoters remembering the last few songs, the long tail of early slots being used for warm-up acts: the lottery says the slot matters, not which of these is the reason.
  • Nothing causal about producer years. Since 2013 producers place the songs, and they have said they do it to pace the show. The 2013–25 numbers are shown for comparison only; a song placed late by a producer may simply be a song the producer rated.
  • Not about any one song. The effect is about one and a half places on average, against a spread of the whole scoreboard. Plenty of openers have won: three in the lottery years.
  • Not a ruling on slot two. Slot two has no winners, and that is unremarkable in aggregate. It does not show slot two is fine either; with this few finals, no single slot's record can show much.
  • Not the whole scoring history. Juries, televotes and points scales changed many times. Working in shares of the field keeps years comparable, but it does not make a 1965 jury and a 2005 televote the same instrument.
  • Wikipedia is the only source. The tables are well kept and pass both parse checks, but an error in an article is an error here. Each figure can be traced to a dated revision.

What did not work

  • 2009–2012 could not be classified. 2007 and 2008 name the countries that chose their slots, so they can be removed. For 2009–12 the articles describe a draw and are silent on whether anyone chose. Rather than guess, those years sit in their own regime and stay out of the headline. Including them makes the effect larger, not smaller, which is a reason for caution and not for comfort.
  • The no-stage control is too small to use. The 1996 qualifying round was judged from tapes in a drawn order, which should separate "being heard late" from "being seen late". It has 29 songs. Its result is shown here for completeness, but a single round cannot tell those apart, and it is not used for anything.
  • The 2007 choosers are evidence, but not data. Eight countries choosing the second half shows what delegations believed. It cannot be tested, because the countries chose; those eight entries are excluded from every test unless you switch them back in above.
